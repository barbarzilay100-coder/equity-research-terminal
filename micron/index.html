The bottom line

  • Micron is in the strongest position in its history. FY2026 revenue was $133.2bn, 3.6 times FY2025, and gross margin reached 86.8% in the fourth quarter.
  • What is new in this cycle is 26 multi-year take-or-pay contracts, backed by $32bn of customer commitments. They make part of today's profits harder to lose.
  • At $1,097, the market already assumes Micron keeps earning about $53–79 per share a year after the boom. That is close to the FY2026 record and 5–7 times the last cycle's peak, while the contracts guarantee only about 14% of the current revenue pace.
  • Hold. I would add on proof that the contracts hold as new supply arrives in 2027, and sell on signs that memory prices are turning.

What Micron makes

Micron is one of three companies, with Samsung and SK hynix, that make almost all of the world's DRAM. It sells two kinds of memory.

DRAM · 73% of FQ4 revenue $39.8bn

The fast working memory every processor needs. Includes HBM, stacks of DRAM placed right next to AI accelerators.

NAND · 26% of FQ4 revenue $14.1bn

Storage that keeps data with the power off, sold mostly as SSDs. Data center SSDs were over two-thirds of NAND revenue.

Is this time different?

A year ago, Micron's annual report described its customers this way:

“Due to volatile industry conditions, our customers are generally reluctant to enter into long-term, fixed-price purchase contracts.” Form 10-K, FY2025

That has changed. Micron has now signed 26 Strategic Customer Agreements (SCAs).

Type
Take-or-pay. The customer pays for a minimum volume even if it buys less.
Term
Through 2030. Two have been extended into 2031.
Pricing
Three-quarters of the expected revenue has a set pricing framework, mostly bands with a floor and a ceiling. The rest is priced to market.
Customer commitments
$32bn, mostly cash deposits. Micron received $12.7bn of deposits in FY2026.
Remaining performance obligations
About $150bn. This is the committed minimum: minimum volumes at minimum prices.

How much is actually locked in

~$33bn / yr contracted minimum$246bn / yr revenue pace

Management estimates the SCAs at over 35% of revenue through 2030, at expected prices. The firm commitment is smaller. Spread over about 4.5 years, $150bn is roughly $33bn a year, about 14% of the $246bn annual pace in Micron's guidance for next quarter ($61.5bn × 4).

New supply arrives from 2027

Early 2027Singapore HBM packaging plant, first output
Mid-2027Idaho fab ID1, first wafers; Tongluo, Taiwan, meaningful shipments
Late 2028Idaho fab ID2 and Japan DRAM expansion, first output; Singapore NAND in the second half
2030New York fab, first wafers

Micron expects the industry to stay short of supply through 2028 and says it has “no line of sight” to when supply and demand return to balance. Its two rivals are adding capacity too, and all of this new supply lands inside the contract term.

What the price assumes

Memory stocks look cheapest at the top of the cycle, because earnings are at a peak. So the useful question is what the price requires once the boom ends.

  1. Guidance for next quarter implies EPS of about $153 a year ($38.15 × 4).
  2. Assume that pace lasts one to two more years. That adds $153–305 per share.
  3. The rest of the price, $792–945, has to be paid for by normal earnings afterwards. At 12–15 times earnings, a typical multiple for a mature chip maker mid-cycle, that means $53–79 per share a year.

The price needs post-boom earnings near FY2026's record, 5–7 times the previous cycle's peak. This is simple reverse math, undiscounted, and not a DCF.

Verdict

HOLD

The business is excellent and the contracts are a real change. But the price already pays for the shortage lasting, and the contracts cover only a small part of current revenue. The next four quarters will show whether prices hold as new supply arrives.

Upgrade to Buy if

  • The FY2026 10-K confirms the $150bn of obligations and the deposits
  • Contract coverage rises toward half of revenue
  • Prices and gross margin hold as the 2027 fabs ramp

Downgrade to Sell if

  • Gross margin falls two quarters in a row. The FQ1-27 guide of 85.95% (GAAP) is already the first guided decline
  • A customer renegotiates a contract or gets a deposit back
  • Capex rises again while price increases slow
